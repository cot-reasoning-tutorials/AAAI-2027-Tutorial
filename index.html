Overview

A systematic map of chain-of-thought reasoning

Six sessions trace CoT from its origins to the research frontier — mechanisms, languages, modalities, faithfulness, and efficiency.

Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning has become a central approach to enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs), evolving from early prompting methods that elicit short intermediate rationales to recent reasoning models that generate long and complex reasoning traces. These advances have opened new research directions while raising fundamental questions about how reasoning works, how it transfers across languages and modalities, whether generated rationales faithfully reflect model behavior, and how reasoning can be made more efficient.

This tutorial provides a systematic overview of the foundations, recent advances, and emerging frontiers of CoT reasoning. We begin with the evolution and taxonomy of CoT, then explore five research directions: mechanisms (explicit, implicit, and latent reasoning), cross-lingual transfer and low-resource languages, cross-modal grounding in images and video, faithful and verifiable rationales, and efficient inference. Each module presents motivation, representative methods, key findings, and open research problems.

The tutorial is intended for researchers and practitioners working on LLMs, natural language processing, reasoning models, multimodal learning, and trustworthy AI, as well as students interested in CoT reasoning. Familiarity with basic machine learning, deep learning, and LLM concepts is recommended, but no prior expertise in CoT reasoning is required.

How does CoT work?

Explicit, implicit, and latent reasoning — and what mechanistic evidence reveals about model behavior.

Where does it transfer?

Cross-lingual reasoning from high- to low-resource languages, and cross-modal grounding in images and video.

Can we trust it — and afford it?

Faithful, verifiable rationales alongside adaptive computation and reasoning compression.

Program

Six sessions, one coherent story

Six 35-minute sessions — 210 minutes of instruction plus a 30-minute break. Each session pairs foundational framing with representative methods and open research directions. Clock times will be posted once the AAAI-27 program is released.

  1. 01
    35 min Libo Qin & Min Zhang

    Background and recent advances

    We define CoT reasoning in LLMs, trace its origins in intermediate natural-language rationales, and review its impact on arithmetic, symbolic, commonsense, and code reasoning. A recent taxonomy organizes methods by sample design (manual, automatic, self-generated), reasoning process (single pass, refinement, search, verification), and reasoning structure (linear, tree, graph, program), connecting short-rationale prompting to long-CoT and reasoning-model paradigms.

    Open questions: process supervision, evaluation beyond final-answer accuracy, benchmark contamination, robustness to noisy rationales, and adaptive test-time compute.
  2. 02
    35 min Liangming Pan

    Mechanisms of CoT reasoning

    We frame the “black-box” challenge by distinguishing explicit CoT (verbalized intermediate steps), implicit reasoning (computation without a written trace), and latent CoT (reasoning in continuous hidden states). We examine reasoning-path aggregation, latent reasoning, and behavioural tests of stepwise computation, together with analysis tools such as logit lenses, attention inspection, causal interventions, activation patching, and controlled task construction.

    Open questions: theoretical accounts of SFT and RL, causal identification of reusable reasoning circuits, verbal–latent interactions, and model-control objectives for hallucination mitigation.
  3. 03
    35 min Libo Qin

    Cross-lingual CoT reasoning

    We separate the language of the question, demonstrations, rationale, and answer — a formulation that explains why reasoning ability varies across languages and why low-resource settings pose challenges beyond translation quality. Using multilingual prompting and the MGSM benchmark, we compare direct multilingual reasoning, translate-then-reason, reason-then-translate, pivot-language reasoning, language-neutral logical representations, multilingual self-consistency, and alignment of reasoning trajectories.

    Open questions: separating reasoning transfer from translation effects, controlling tokenizer and script differences, culturally grounded evaluation, and consistency when valid explanations differ across languages.
  4. 30 min

    Coffee break

  5. 04
    35 min Hao Fei

    Cross-modal CoT reasoning

    For visual understanding, structured intermediate steps can bridge language with spatial and temporal evidence in images and videos. We distinguish failures of perception from failures of inference, then cover two-stage rationale-and-answer models, event- and object-centric reasoning, structured intermediate representations, interleaved visual–text traces, symbolic multimodal logic, and temporally grounded video reasoning.

    Open questions: long-horizon video reasoning, multimodal reasoning agents, fine-grained evidence attribution, visual-token efficiency, and counterfactual evaluation.
  6. 05
    35 min Zhuosheng Zhang

    Faithful CoT reasoning

    A fluent rationale can be plausible without reporting the factors that actually caused a prediction. We separate final-answer correctness, step-level validity, evidence grounding, causal faithfulness, calibration, and consistency between a stated plan and the actions taken. Methods include counterfactual tests, perturbations and causal interventions, process and outcome verification, uncertainty estimation, and symbolic translation and checking.

    Open questions: scalable supervision when gold reasoning processes are unavailable, human-in-the-loop escalation, adversarial rationales, and faithfulness in multi-agent settings.
  7. 06
    35 min Yafu Li

    Efficient CoT reasoning

    Large reasoning models can repeat steps, overthink, explore failed search branches, and misallocate computation. Following a model-lifecycle taxonomy, we cover concise data and supervision, adaptive inference budgets, routing and early stopping, step- and token-level CoT compression, distillation into shorter or latent traces, continuous reasoning, SFT, and length-aware RL — evaluating efficiency jointly with accuracy and reliability rather than rationale length alone.

    Open questions: Loop Transformers, continuous thought, reliable stopping criteria, hardware-aware evaluation, and Pareto reporting over tokens, latency, memory, energy, and task success.
